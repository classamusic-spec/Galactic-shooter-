# The Ophiuchus Reach — Story Bible

The campaign spine, the faction logic, and the rule set that keeps five worlds
reading as one war instead of five arenas. Anything that puts words in front of
the player — mission names, objective lines, briefings, toasts, weapon flavour,
enemy names — is bound by this document.

## The premise, in one paragraph

Eleven days ago the Federation's survey network in the Ophiuchus Reach went
silent. Five worlds, five species, none of which have ever cooperated with
another. You are a Vanguard operative sent to find out why they suddenly are.

## The spine

The five worlds are not a menu. Each one answers the question the previous world
asked, and asks the next. That is the whole structure, and it is the reason the
player travels.

| # | World | Question it answers | Question it asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Aurvangr** | What happened to the survey teams? | Who armed the Jötunn with technology they cannot build? |
| 2 | **Zeta Reticuli IV** | The Custodians armed no one — their Archive was raided. | What was taken, and by whom? |
| 3 | **Khepri** | The stolen thing is being tested on living worlds. | What does it do when it finishes? |
| 4 | **Hive Prime** | This. The Unnumbered are a finished world. | Who is holding the trigger? |
| 5 | **Draco IX** | The Saurian Legions, with a fleet aimed at Federation space. | — |

## The Choir

The thing at the centre. Kept deliberately spare: it is more frightening as a
process than as a monster, and a process is cheaper to render than a god.

The Custodians catalogued it long before the Federation existed and did not
destroy it, because their whole civilisation is built on cataloguing rather than
acting — a failure of nerve dressed up as principle. The Choir is a resonance:
seeded into a biosphere, it makes everything alive there converge. Individual
minds thin out. The population stops being a set of creatures and becomes a
count. Hive Prime is what a world looks like eighteen months after seeding.

The Saurian Legions stole the Choir's core from the Archive. They do not
understand it. They believe they are wielding a weapon; they are carrying a
disease with a delivery schedule.

**Rule: never explain the Choir in a cutscene.** The player learns it by seeing
Khepri half-turned and Hive Prime finished, in that order. Every line about it is
observation, never exposition.

## The five factions

Each faction needs one sentence a player could repeat after one mission.

- **Jötunn Clans** (Aurvangr) — *Mercenaries who took a contract they did not
  read.* Proud, close-range, ritual. They are not villains; they were paid in
  weapons they could not make and did not ask where they came from. The only
  faction you could imagine negotiating with, which is why they open the game.
- **The Custodians** (Zeta Reticuli IV) — *Archivists who catalogued the end of
  the world and filed it.* Cold, precise, ranged, technological. Hostile because
  you are contamination in their record, not because they hate you. Their crime
  is inaction.
- **Bladed Broods** (Khepri) — *Predators being rewritten mid-hunt.* Fast,
  ambushing, vertical. Some are still themselves. Some are not. The player should
  be able to tell which by how they fight.
- **The Unnumbered** (Hive Prime) — *Not a faction. A result.* No leadership, no
  territory, no negotiation. The only enemy in the game with no individual name
  anywhere in its roster — that absence is the characterisation.
- **Saurian Legions** (Draco IX) — *A war machine holding something it does not
  understand.* Disciplined, heavy, armoured, organised. The most conventional
  enemy in the game, deliberately: after Hive Prime the player needs something
  they can actually fight.

## Voice

- **The Federation is not clean.** It armed the Reach a generation ago and lost
  track of what it sold. The Custodians' archive names Federation buyers. This is
  raised and never resolved, because a war does not pause for it.
- **No one calls you a hero.** Handler traffic is terse, procedural and slightly
  overworked. Praise is rationed to twice in the whole campaign.
- **Sentence length is the mood dial.** Briefings run long; mid-firefight lines
  run under eight words.
- **The Choir gets no dialogue, no theme, no face.** It is only ever described by
  what it leaves behind.

## Handler

**Vanguard Actual** — the voice in your ear. Never seen. Runs six operations at
once and it shows. Gives you the objective, the reason, and nothing else. In the
Hive Prime briefing they stop talking mid-sentence and come back thirty seconds
later; that is the only time the campaign lets them react.

## Mission naming

Two-word titles, no colons, no articles. `Cold Contract`, `Silent Archive`,
`Green Rot`, `The Count`, `Iron Choir`. Chapter numbers appear only in the star
map, never in-world.

## Rules for anyone writing text into this game

1. **Objective lines are instructions first.** A player glancing at the HUD
   mid-firefight must learn what to do from the first three words. Flavour goes
   after the verb, or nowhere.
2. **No word the player cannot act on.** "Sever the resonance lattice" is only
   allowed if there is a lattice on screen with a health bar.
3. **Never name the Choir before Zeta Reticuli.** Aurvangr's script does not know
   the word.
4. **Every faction gets exactly one idea.** Do not give the Jötunn a secret
   sympathetic sub-plot; they are the contract, and that is enough.
5. **Cut every adjective you can lose.** The setting is doing the work.
